ReCollect64 Reviews No. 116

Star Fox [NS2] ~ Nov 23, 2026

Honorable Histories

Star Fox is a wonderfully enduring Nintendo exclusive franchise. Since the first game's inception on the Super Nintendo, Fox McCloud and his mercenary squad of pilots have been rescuing planets and recounting their tales of battles against the evil antagonist, Andross, for over three decades. Where the N64 version, Lylat Wars/Star Fox 64 (1997), expanded the lore on the 1993 SNES game Star Wing/Star Fox, and Star Fox 3D (2011) was a remake of the N64 iteration for the stereoscopic handheld, and, in turn Star Fox Zero (2016) was a reimagining of the core Lylat Wars story for the Wii U; Star Fox (2026) for the Nintendo Switch 2 is yet another retelling of the core story but with a fresh art direction.

I recall playing the first game on the SNES and being blown away by the pseudo-3D graphics at the time. I was enthralled by the wireframe models, the slick animations, and the vocal camaraderie between the crew. The latest remake of a remake of a remake starring the heroic galactic team was on my radar from the second it was announced in May 2026, and having picked it up at release just one month later in June and absolutely hammered it, I have been quietly sitting on a rough draft of this review, pondering if it was right for my site. With the recent release of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, I feel like it's the perfect time to start covering any and every N64 game that gets remade for the latest Nintendo home consoles.

Nostalgically Nuanced

Having replayed and reviewed Lylat Wars/Star Fox 64 back in March, I was eager to see just how this game might improve or perhaps deviate from the already stellar original. I can gleefully confirm that 99.9% of everything a Lylat Wars/Star Fox 64 fan could hope for has made the transition fully intact, and that there has been heaps more added besides!

The core layout, characters and story are, for all intents and purposes, exactly the same as you would expect them to be. Following the disbanding of the original Star Fox team featuring James McCloud, Peppy Hare and Pigma Dengar, the Lylat system is in danger from a full-scale attack by Andross and his evil forces. Forming a fresh team, Star Fox, Peppy Hare, Falco Lombardi and Slippy Toad embark on a series of reconnaisance and exploratory missions to protect and enforce peace under General Pepper of the Cornerian Defence Force.

With the extra grunt of the latest Nintendo hardware at our finger tips, Velan Studios have endevoured to expand the lore and add-in additional cinematics to revitalise the story even more than the N64 remake ever could for the SNES original. We now see the story of Pigma's betrayal, and we get deeper insights into characters' backstories before, during and after each level, fleshing out mission briefings and expanding upon the team building that is underway with a more youthful and inexperienced Fox McCloud at the helm.

(Graphically bold, the level of detail and cinematic impact of the NS2 edition of Star Fox is outstanding)

Barrel Rolling

Starting with a tutorial level that explains everything from basic manoeuvres to power-ups, Yaru De Pon, the trainer and Arwing mechnic Racoon-Dog, returns as the voice that guides you. Performing your various moves is simple, with the main A, B, X and Y buttons corresponding to laser cannon fire, breaking, boosting and bombing respectively. The L, R, Zl and ZR buttons control your ship's tilt and roll, and double tapping any of those performs the iconic barrel roll that deflects enemy fire.

Where the C-buttons have gone from the controller, the D-pad is now home to your other tricks, such as performing a U-turn or a somersault. The controls feel smooth and precise, with aerial manoeuvres feeling slick and responsive on the Joy-Con 2s as well as on the Pro Controller, and aiming with analogue or gyroscopic controls and firing with the tap of a button feel equally superb when you're in the thick of it. I have to say that had the controls not been almost 1:1 with the N64 version, there would have been hell to pay. Thankfully, these are spot-on!

All of the 16 levels are fully featured here, with the waterscapes and city-like areas of Corneria, the snowy wilderness of Fichina (previously known as Fortuna), the rolling waves of Zoness ocean and Solar's incandescent magma, and the submerged depths of Aquas all looking exquisitely lit, highly detailed and visually stunning. Reaching each of these takes special know-how, so you'll probably only play 7 levels per run, which means it will take more than one play through, usually 3-4, to unlock every level and see every secret route. I love the fact that to get to Titania, you have to let Slippy get smashed by Spyborg, and that to get to Fichina, you still need to do certain things on Corneria or Meteo. Thankfully, for the uninitiated, there are a lot of hints in the challenge mode, for which there are 192 to complete and 32 medals to collect!

Notably, the Blue Marine and Landmaster levels feel more atmospheric and weighty too. For example, when chasing the train on Macbeth the controls feel tighter and more akin to driving a heavily armoured tank. I particularly enjoyed this level, especially with its multiple outcomes, but even the Aquas level was more appealing to play thanks to the revised snappier controls and highly polished, technically lush environments.

(Each biome feels unique and massive. Lens flare, heat haze, explosions, smoke, fog, lighting and shadow all add to the feeling of immersion)

Space Animals

Each character has a slightly different voice from that which I was accustomed to. Naturally there are new voice actors who give their spin on each of the characters. Peppy has a big more of a southern twang, Biull has somewhat of a less southern twang, Slippy sounds slightly more mature somehow, Katt has a less sultry vibe, Falco retains his glib cockiness, Andross has a more animated inflection to his vocals, and General Pepper, James McCloud, and Fox McCloud all manage to keep quite authentic tones to them; however, ROB 64 has been completely re-voiced and sounds overly nerdy in my opinion. Overall though, the characters do sound great and have a strong presence as well as distinct individuality within their personalities.

By adding more character and injecting additional scenes or moments where the characters react, I feel that the game has been enhanced and bolstered in that which we already loved about the game. I could see how some players might find these additions unnecessary or like they've been crammed in to fill gaps, but I honestly think these extra scenes build a better story all-told.

If there is one thing I'm thankful for, it's that the team behind this game didn't produce it with the Fox McCloud character we saw in The Super Mario Galaxy Movie. That version of Fox was, in my opinion, campy, loud and obnoxious, which isn't the direction Velan Studios have taken with him. Our protagonist here is a more realistic, less cartoony anthropomorphic fox. If the game had been given an over-the-top brash comedic twist, I probably would have been quite thrown by it and not enjoyed this game half as much as I did with the serious yet headstrong characterisation.

(There are lots of secret routes and bonus things to collect via the challenge mode. Boss fights are screen-filling and imposing)

Additional Action

Have you ever thought, "What would the original Lylat Wars have been like if it had featured cooperative play?" Well, wonder no more. This version allows you to team up to experience a two-player campaign and challenge modes for maximum teamwork and destruction. This mode is fantastically exhilarating, especially for that "Top Gun" feeling of having a wingman (or woman) at your side.

In addition to the regular 4-player split-screen mode, there is also now an online mode whereby you can have up to 8 players in a 4v4 dogfight as Star Fox vs. Star Wolf. There are 6 levels to compete across (as of the September update), and you have various objectives to tick off, such as capturing zones, gathering crystals or stealing cargo, as well as the traditional deathmatch dog-fighting.

On Nintendo Switch 2, when playing online, you can use a webcam to have your face integrated in-game. In Star Fox in particular, you can have a V-tuber-like avatar superimposed or recognisable accessories augmented onto your face as you trash talk your adversaries. I love how the full face filters mouths open and close to match yours, eyes blink along with yours, and your head movement is fully tracked too. The silly little augmented accessories, like a set of Katt's pink ears are hilariously comical touches too. There are 25 character avatars to unlock, 18 AR accessories to collect as well as infinitely customisable battle mode banners and a holoviewer database to complete too. No ne could ever say that they didnt add much, because there's plenty to enjoy on or offline!

The Pros:

 Incredible graphics that bring every area to life.
 Rock solid cinematics that add to the story and lore.
 Additional cooperative and online versus modes are excellent.
 192 challenges and 32 medals to collect.
 You can play with mouse controls!
 A remade and rearranged musical score with new tracks too!
 Avatars for online play are brilliant and hilarious.
 Amiibo support to unlock stuff early.
The Cons:

 No online leaderboard for high scores.
 The campaign is still a bit short; a new level or two would have been great!
Conclusion:

I absolutely adore what Velan Studios has done with Star Fox. Not only have they faithfully used the base material to make a familiar yet modern edition, but they have also brought some fantastic multiplayer additional content into the fray, making this easily the definitive way to experience the Lylat Wars story, characters and camaraderie to date, either docked on your big-screen TV or on the move in handheld mode!
