Xibalba 64 ~ Jul 28, 2026

eXamining Xibalba 64

With ModRetro's M64 and the first four accompanying games releasing on July 28th, Dominic Szablewski (PhobosLab) kindly hooked me up with a copy of Xibalba 64 early so that I could give it the full ReCollect64 official review treatment!

Modern Mayan Marvel

Xibalba 64 is one of the first original M64 titles to be released with the system, and as such it comes presented in a box with a gorgeously glossy sleek black physical cartridge and a wonderfully informative miniature manual too.

The essence of this game is that you explore the often maze-like Mayan underworld, searching for the path to Xibalba, defying the gods and honourably reaching the afterlife. It's a strange story but compelling nonetheless, and the game gifts you philosophical, spiritual, and intriguingly powerful messages before each level, in place of cutscenes or cinematics.

With 17 levels to explore alone or cooperatively, trophies and unlockables hidden within them, and 2-4 player deathmatches across 12 of those environments, there is a heck of a lot of gameplay to enjoy here.

The game features multiple options for sound, screen modes and even a texture filter for softening those jaggies. Having said that, I preferred the unfiltered raw square-screened look; it appears far more authentic to the Nintendo 64's hardware regardless whether you are playing on an OG console or an FPGA device.

Beginning the game, I immediately mapped player movement onto the stick's up and down directions with left and right to turn and proceeded to use the C-buttons only to strafe. This setup felt a little more natural for me, rather than using the C-buttons to move and strafe a la Turok.

Block Blasters

The stylisation of Xibalba 64 is exceptionally polished. There are definite visual nods to Doom, Quake, and Wolfenstein, and there is a looming, dark, brooding and foreboding overall feel to the game. The blocky, cubic layouts, geometry and the dappled colour palettes across every surface ensure that the game runs flawlessly, even on real hardware. I particularly like the hanging chains and vines that drape over you as you push through them; it's a really nice interactive element that immerses you deeper into the role you play. There are lighting effects that alter the look and feel of each space as they change, and special flooring effects such as ice and lava that add yet more layers of danger to your adventure.

While collecting keys to enter new places and hunting for breakable walls to expose secrets, there are several enemy types in this game blocking your path, each with varied attack patterns and increasing levels of viciosness. The main grunts are the Splitjaws; they jump and nip at you but don't pose too much of a threat. Working up to teleporting Shamans and Tech Shamans, hulking brutish Vinebackers (ice variants too), powerful stone golems, and projectile-spewing spider-like tanks - these enemies are trickier forces to be reckoned with, though thankfully each one can be dealt with succinctly enough as long as you attack back with the correct weaponry and keep strafing. I noticed that some enemies try to warp ahead of you, so backing into a corner can force them to reveal themselves on your terms. Others always fire from their left side, so you can use the corner of a wall as cover while you pummel their right side.

Weapons in this game are intense and violent, yet quite few and far between with just 5 weapons to use across the 17 sprawling environments. You have a basic rechargeable plasmagun, an eviscerating shotgun, a blisteringly fast-firing minigun (my absolute fave), a rocket launcher and a grenade launcher. Each one has their own pros and cons, for example, the rechargeable rifle takes time to recharge when it runs dry, unless you pick up additional ammo; the shotgun has a reload time between each shot; the minigun absolutely burns through ammunition but shreds; and the grenade launcher, while able to bounce and ricochet off walls, is extremely lethal to you as well as the NPCs, so don't neglect your health, and keep picking up those medipaks!

The music in Xibalba 64 is stunningly atmospheric throughout. I love the thumping menu music, and each level has a looping pulse-like rhythm to it that is just as moody as the environments. The overall nostalgia of the visuals, the controls and the sounds make for one well-rounded and fully engrossing package that feels like so much more than the sum of its parts.

Dungeon Delights

As a massive FPS fan, I thoroughly enjoyed this game from start to finish. The style and explorative nature of Xibalba 64 are extremely inviting. It harks back to a formative era of gameplay, managing to fully emulate those early 90s shooters while delivering its own brand of style and violence. It could almost be titled as a "dungeon crawler shooter" too, with winding mazes and multitudes of doors, keys, and secrets to discover.

Beating it on normal wasn't too much of an issue, running me around for approximately 4 hours in total. Beating it on the harder difficulties proved far more challenging and took a good while longer with plenty more deaths incurred than I care to have on public record.

A couple of things I felt were missing were some kind of checkpoint system and an in-game map. Once or twice I got totally turned around in the game, especially in the level "Recovery Protocol". The level is so dark and so complex that I could really have used a Turok-style map overlay to figure out where I had been and where I hadn't visited yet. Finding those switches in the dark was quite taxing, especially when, once I had found the third and final switch, I had my last remaining sliver of health pulverised by a turret and had to start the level in its entirety all over again.

I enjoy the trial and error approach, but perhaps a map would take away from the explorative core and save points would diminish the difficulty, so I can understand why these features were not included to some extent.

Multiplayer Madness

Infestation is a great way to let off some steam, especially if you have unlocked a load of levels and a heap of cheats. Enabling all guns and infinite ammo let you absolutely storm the place; however, your bragging rights go out the window as the game logs whether or not cheats were used, so it's wise to only activate them at your leisure and never competitively, obviously.

The widescreen modes make a huge difference for screen space in multiplayer. Playing the main game in 4:3 feels right, but I think split-screen feels a lot nicer with more visual real estate available. I have to admit that Deathmatch, Coop and Infestation are an absolute riot when experienced with friends. It takes me back to the N64's glory days.

The fact that Xibalba 64 also champions exploration in the campaign, in order to open up more multiplayer content, is a testament to Dominic's understanding of what made the 64-bit system so great. The Nintendo 64 specialised in crowded sofas and people huddling around a TV into the early hours. I get this same raw feeling from playing this game that I used to get back in the pre-Y2K era; the nostalgia is more than palpable; it lives in games like these.

Another small thing I would have loved to have seen is increasingly daunting enemies in the infestation mode. I understand why the pesky little Splitjaws were used as the main foe here; they slowly but surely overrun the maps and cause you no end of bother nipping at your heels without feeling overpowered until your collectively overwhelmed. I think it would have been great to see these enemies increase in stature and difficulty as your hit count increases into the hundreds. It's such a minor quibble, but I think it could have made an already great extra mode all the more challenging, especially given the variety of enemies the main game contains.

The Pros:

 17 levels, 12 arenas, 9 cheats and 5 weapons.
 Stunningly stylised graphics and enemies.
 3-4 hours of single-player gameplay.
 2-player cooperative campaign.
 1-4 player infestation and deathmatch modes.
 Runs immaculately on OG N64 and FPGA devices.
The Cons:

 No in-game map or progress checkpoints.
 Infestation could be more varied.
Conclusion:

Xibalba 64 is a huge evolution from its web-browser roots with more levels, more puzzles, more enemies, more weapons and more action than ever before.

Though the core game can be beaten in around 3-4 hours, there is enough replayability, secrets, maps and multiplayer modes to keep you coming back for more and inviting friends over to join in with the Mayan mayhem.

Could we see more PhobosLab's titles such as Voidcall, XType Plus or even Underrun come to the M64 too? I sure hope so!
