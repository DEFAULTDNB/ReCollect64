Donut Dodo ~ Oct 1, 2026

Genre De-Extinction

It's 2026, and the Nintendo 64 still gets new physical game releases! Donut Dodo (2022), by Flynn's Arcade and Pixel Games, is a retro-inspired, light-hearted arcade-style platformer that pits you, Baker Billy Burns, against the naughty titular Dodo. Gathering your baked goods as fast as you can, and in the best order possible to maximise your bonus points, Donut Dodo embodies endorphin-fuelled frustration, but yet it is incredibly moreish.

With heavy influences from the likes of Nintendo's smash-hit Donkey Kong (1981), this game takes a very similar devilishly simple formula, gives it modern seasoning with achievements and extra modes, and floated the idea of an N64 iteration on Kickstarter, which was fully funded in under 24 hours!

Dastardly Dodo

Starting the game, you have a 90-second timer to collect your small circular snacks and then nab the last giant sugary treat off the titular flightless foe. The game is simple: dodge the Dodo and his horde of minons and obstacles, and collect everything, including cherries, as swiftly as you can. It sounds rather chilled, but it really isn't. Learning the patterns, jumping and avoiding the flames, rats and slurping toilets (which are like the ghosts in Pac-Man), is quite challenging, even on the beginner difficulties.

The glazed goods glow and shake at certain points too. Collecting them while they're in this glitzy mode allows you to score yet more points. Ensuring your path is clear to get them whilst avoiding flanking attacks from above, below, and both sides, if you can collect all of them in this enhanced mode, you guessed it; you score more points! Donut Dodo is a tricky game to master and an even trickier game to put down because you'll want to achieve that illusive perfect run and see your name up in pixels in the hall of fame!

This game is tough, really tough. There aren't even "continues". But that is because it's a 2D arcade title. Arcades, in the days of yore, thrived on people feeding coins into their machines, so as long as you appreciate the simplistic but rock-solid gameplay loop and the path to excellence costing you countless restarts; you will have a fantastic time bopping along to the retro beats and besting the feathered fiend. The dodo itself kind of reminds me of the Cadbury's Parrot. I don't know if I'm showing my age now, but the resemblance is striking!

A Tasty Treat

With a marathon mode named "Endless Delights" and "Sugar Rush", a speedrun mode, in addition to the main game, the console content and presentation are immaculate. From the beautiful chiptune soundtrack by Sean Bialo to the multitude of 16-colour CRT-tuned pixel animations and levels that really pop and draw you in to the gameplay. The environments are effortlessly detailed and meticulously devised to make it a real game of cat and mouse as you hunt down the goodies and avoid the baddies.

Though each stage is a single-screen affair, the game really evokes the feeling that this is an arcade machine being played on your N64. There are options in the settings menus that add can offset the HUD, enable rumble and remove screen flashes, but the N64 version saldy doesn't contain CRT filters. I achieved this by running my copy on the Analogue 3D. Horizontal up-scaling and scan-line filters looks superb with this game, but for the purposes of the screenshots I used the clean setting as they compressed better when viewed online.

There are two options for the physical release: a regular boxed grey cart (£65/£52 early bird) or the boxed DODO-RED edition cart (£214). There was also the coveted "Executive Producer" edition, which comes on a one-off gold-coloured cartridge (£1282) and puts your name in the credits, but it has been snapped up by one lucky (wealthy) fan. All of the above are region free (though you can specify PAL or NTSC-U style packaging) and will come with a manual too, so if you feel like dabbling in a new physical release for your console, Pixel Games has you covered. If you're a digital kind of player, then the ROM download option is just £9 and works perfectly on flashcarts, emulators and FPGA systems too!

The Pros:

 Brilliantly responsive controls.
 Multiple difficuly levels.
 5 levels, plus a bonus stage.
 4 modes including marathon.
 12 achievements to unlock.
 Wonderful chiptune soundtrack.
 Vibrant 16-colour design.
 Rumble Pak support.
 EEPROM saves for cart owners.
The Cons:

 Tough to beat on higher difficulties.
 No multiplayer modes.
 Only contains 6 levels in total.
Conclusion:

With a constant pull to achieve higher scores every time, Donut Dodo is a fun and well-polished game that will get your blood pumping on the 64-bit system.

Some may be quick to dismiss this as an old-looking game that "isn't even 3D", but with gameplay, graphics and sound like this, it has more than enough nostalgic charm to keep true players hooked for a good while!
