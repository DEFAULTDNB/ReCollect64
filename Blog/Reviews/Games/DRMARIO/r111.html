Dr. Mario 64

Qualification Quandry

Mario Mario (yes, that's his surname) is notoriously a plumber, right? So then how is he also a doctor? Dr Mario 64 is an increasingly fast-paced puzzler designed to get you killing viruses with kindness through the flu season by landing matching vertically tumbling "Megavitamin" capsules on them.

Released for the Nintendo 64 in the Americas in 2001 and again in 2003 for the Chinese-exclusive iQue player, Dr. Mario 64 was a remake of the 1990 NES and Game Boy title which never made it to the UK shores until it came to the Nintendo Switch Online service in 2021, skipping the Wii and Wii U virtual console era entirely.

I picked up the NTSC-U cartridge a number of years ago, but I never really sought to give it a check-up until now!

Virus Replication

At its core, Dr Mario 64 is a vertical-dropping tile-matching game that requires you to connect and destroy a series of randomly generated viruses in a medicine bottle. The idea is to match four identical coloured pieces in a line, horizontally or vertically, inclusive of the same coloured virus to pop them off this mortal coil. Matching 4 seems simple, but each vitamin capsule has two coloured segments, so sometimes you have to sacrifice one end to make the others match together. The result is junk blocks that clutter your screen and hamper your virus-busting progress.

There are several modes to this game, but none are overly distinct or have enough variation for my liking. Single player (classic) is as described above but using the entire screen for just one player. Story mode pushes you through "Dr Mario and The Cold Caper", whereby you go around the village (stage by stage) fixing patients head-to-head on screen against Wario, who wants your Megavitamins, and progressing to a boss-fight of sorts with Rudy the Clown, an obscure character from an old Wario game I have clearly never played, and culminating in a final face-off against a secret character dependon on whether you're playing as Mario or Wario.

VS Computer is like single-player but playing against the CPU. Flash mode is like VS Computer mode, whereby you must defeat 3 flashing viruses first. Marathon mode is where the bottle fills from the bottom and contains the most viruses to tackle and therefore the most challenge. Lastly, in terms of single-player modes, there is Score Attack, which asks you to get the highest score possible within a 3-minute window of opportunity. Honestly, they're all just too similar to one another to offer an exciting range of challenges.

Multiplayer mode is only selectable once more than one controller is connected and allows for up to four of you to face off against each other. This is a great mode for party play, so I highly recommend this mode if you can muster up four controllers and four people to sit around your TV.

Overdosed & Underwhelming

The game controls almost exclusively with the D-pad and the A button, which means that the analogue stick is completely redundant. The Z-trigger, which could have been utilised to dump your piece to the bottom of the screen faster, is also criminally unused. I found that sometimes the controls felt unresponsive. As the pieces came down faster and faster, the input didn't feel sharp enough to allow for the game to be played to the best of its abilities, and I tested both the Analogue 3D with the 8BitDo 64 controller and with an OEM controller, and I tried this game with the OG hardware and an OEM controller for clarity.

Featuring just two unlockable characters, and they're strange additions at that. Vampire Wario and Metal Mario are added to the roster should you clear the game with either Dr Mario or Wario, respectively, with no losses. Neither of these characters adds any value to the game once unlocked, which is disheartening; it would have been good if they had unique abilities, or if they could be used to unlock more characters in turn, but alas, no, they're just skins. You can't even unlock Rudy, which is even more of a letdown in my honest opinion.

Playing two players, the game somehow feels more authentic, albeit still hampered by inaccurate controls. I enjoyed playing this mode far more than the majority of the single-player modes, though I have to say that Marathon mode was also a notably decent mode. I could imagine that playing 4-player mode would be extremely enjoyable too, because the trash-talking and interactivity of dumping blocks onto other players' screens would be all the more heightened against human players.

The Pros:

 Fast-paced puzzling action.
 1-4 player action.
 2 additional characters to unlock.
 Colourful & vibrant with funky music.
The Cons:

 Throwaway storyline.
 Modes feel too similar.
 Unresponsive controls.
Conclusion:

As matching/dropping games go this isn't too bad I guess, but the ghosty controller inputs and indestinct modes leave me wanting something more from a Mario title. This one seriously underwhelms in most departments.

If the controls were tighter and modes more condensed and refined I think this could have been an essential title, because the idea is great, it's just the execution that feels half-arsed.
