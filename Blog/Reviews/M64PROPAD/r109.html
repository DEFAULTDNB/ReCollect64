M64 Pro Controller ~ Aug 13, 2025
M64 Pro Controller

Professional Paddles

It seems that with every console that has existed in the last 5-10 years, it must be accompanied by its own "Pro" controller. The ModRetro M64 is no exception to this rule, with their very own high-spec pad that is recommended for play to get the most out of your brand-spanking-new FPGA console.

The M64 Pro controller comes in clear, purple, green, red, and smoke (releasing August 21st) and packs in a retro feel with modern components at its core. Based on the OEM N64 controller and taking stick-based inspiration from the updated LodgeNet controllers, and even the iQue player, that proceeded the OEM and provided more robust features, this controller is definitely something I wanted to get my hands on and take for a spin!

With claims such as "Play four-player N64 games with imperceptible lag" of just 0.375 milliseconds and "a 100% linear response mimicking original hardware", these are big boasts, so is the jargon justifiable?

 

Technical Specifications:

+ Cool-touch aluminum shell in the famous trident shape that you grew up on.

+ Wired or wireless connectivity supporting Bluetooth (BLE 6.2) SCI (when officially released by Bluetooth SIG, only with M64 or other SCI compatible devices).

+ Driftless next-gen TMR thumbsticks designed to be user-replaceable (snap-in mount + pogo pin) with ease.

+ Classic style ERM rumble motor built in.

+ Power it your way – rechargeable pack, AA pack, and 3 meter wired options all included.

+ Plug your controller in once, and after that, it wirelessly connects to your M64 automatically.

+ 200 hours batterylife, controller pak emulation (coming soon), web updatable firmware.

+ Compatible with M64, Nintendo 64®, Android, Windows, and Linux.

(The "AMD Red" pro controller matches my "AMD Red" M64 console's colouration, but with a matt finish)

Quality and Quantity

The absolute first thing you notice about this controller is the materials it is composed of. It's no cheap plastic affair; there is a wonderful non-slip ceramic-coated aluminium surface across the rear and a polycarbonate front plate that has a nice level of translucency and a distinctive matte feel.

The stick is a GC-style, TMR (Tunnel Magnetoresistance) device that mitigates any possibility of drift and droop. It feels slick sitting in its default "squashed octagon" gate, but it does come with two additional restrictors, which include one that is perfectly circular and another that is a squared-off octagon. Swapping the gates involves removing 11 screws from the rear of the pad, and of the three, I think possibly the equidistant octagon is slightly nicer for more precise N64-like inputs.

The buttons are proud and responsive. I found that the face buttons were very comfortable under my fingers and performed exactly as I had hoped. The D-pad is a little sharp around the edges, but it works absolutely perfectly (contrary to things I've seen about them reported online). Special mentions go to the triangular concaved shoulder buttons and the wider Z-trigger; these are highly evolved and significantly better than those on the OEM controller, making it feel more comfortable and even easier to locate and press each button without even trying. Pressing "Start + Z" brings up the OSD, and holding start boots up the console, but there is no equally quick way to turn the console off via the controller without going through a heap of menus, which is annoying.

The ways to play are interesting. In the box, you get a 3m braided cable, a rechargeable battery, and a battery caddy for popping in some AAs. The latter is the least useful option and the most environmentally wasteful unless you're using rechargeable batteries. The battery pack itself is a 1250 mAh, 3.7v polymer Li-ion battery, rated for 200+ hours of play, and I would say that after 50+ hours I haven't seen a single shred of movement in the battery indicator, which is extremely impressive.

Additionally, the input lag is absolutely minimal with zero notable drops or ghosting. I couldn't exactly verify it across four controllers, but with the one I purchased, it was extremely responsive throughout my general testing across a vast array of games that I have played with it.

(The benchmarks aren't bad, but the GC-style stick is not ideal)

Controlling Characters

Playing games with the controller is a mixed bag. For the majority of games it performs admirably, but for anything that requires the utmost in precision, you'll need to adjust the sensitivity or swap out the gate to the equidistant square one. I found that the 75% linear response sensitivity setting felt the best for most games, but that may be subjective depending on your personal tastes. A good example of a game that needs precision is GoldenEye 007. Using the sniper rifle at the very beginning of the level is trickier to control, and shooting the padlock or hitting distant headshots or knocking out surveillance cameras while using the on-screen reticle is a bit more taxing.

For other games that don't require overly precise inputs, like Super Mario 64, Diddy Kong Racing, Mario Kart 64, Lylat Wars, Mischief Makers, Killer Instinct Gold, Blast Corps, F-Zero X, Super Smash Bros. (Remix) or anything else, the controller performs brilliantly. It really feels like an elevated version of an official N64 controller, without veering into weird modern tropes in terms of shape and form. I have to appreciate the build quality too. The controller doesn't even creak or flex under pressure; it's built to last for generations, and I have no doubt that it should.

This controller doesn't even strike me as a "third-party" controller. It's too uncompromising to the original ergonomics. The central handle is admittedly a tad longer, but overall it retains the original form and keeps the classic feel on-point, albeit being heavier due to the component materials. At 375 grams, it makes the 200-gram OG controller feel flyweight in comparison. The biggest advantage the OG pad still has is the acessory socket and connectivity to a transfer pak. But after a few updates, the M64 Pro controller will never house a true transfer pak.

(The console and controller match beautifully, and each are rock solid in terms of build-quality)

GC-Style Sticking Point

I have a couple of gripes with this otherwise excellent controller. Firstly, the analogue stick. While it does hold up in tests, it doesn't exactly hold up in-game. I found it felt twitchy and less accurate in games like GoldenEye 007 and Perfect Dark, leaving me thinking that it's a bit too sensitive in comparison to the OEM controller's perfect optical gear gate construction. Secondly, the lack of rumble and memory is absolutely dumbfounding. Sure, the rumble feature has now been added one week on since the console's release, but the lack of a virtual memory pak or way to save anything via save states is infuriating.

As I mentioned in my console review, if someone new to the N64 scene picked up the M64, an M64 Pro controller and a copy of Extreme-G: Turbo Fusion expecting to have a flawless time, they won't be able to save their game, not to mention that they might also find it harder to control than with an OEM pad thanks to the twitchier stick.

The M64 controller is adaptable and interchangeable, but at a cost to the consumer. Additional parts won't be free, and with the pad already weighing in at £82, it might be even more costly to put a TMR N64-style stick back into it (should ModRetro or any third party supplies one) to hopefully resolve the sensitivity and make it even better for N64 purists.

The Pros:

 Evolved OEM shape, feel and buttons are great.
 Non-slip with solid build quality throughout.
 Rechargeable pack & AA battery caddy included.
 Three meter braided cable for wired play.
 Two additional restrictor gates supplied.
 Modular design & easy-to-swap parts.
 On-pad sensitivity adjustment (L/R + Start for 1 second).
 Eccentric Rotating Mass (ERM) rumble feels brilliant.
 LED light ring indicator is a nice touch.
The Cons:

 GC-style stick is overly sensitive.
 No virtual memory pak.
 No virtual rumble pak (initially).
 No supplied cable to update the pad.
Conclusion:

This controller has a lot going for it, but it feels like it has all the gear but only some of the ideas. The TMR sticks are future-proof, but the GC-style stick is not true to the original. The shape, feel and weight are superb, but the lack of support for a memory card day one (within the pad or virtually on the console) is either a thoughtless oversight or the sign of a rushed product.

The included power cable has a nice solid connection to tether it to the controller and console, but obviously, it therefore cannot be used to update the controller, so you need to use an additional data-enabled USB-C cable to connect it. On the plus side, since the 0.12 update arrived on the 13th of August, the M64 Pro controller does now have a "virtual rumble pak", which honestly feels incredible, but again, it is something that should have been included right out of the gates.

Having spent a week with it, I really like what they have done. In my opinion, the stick definitely needs replacing with a thinner, N64-style stick for maximum authenticity and feel. Perhaps once the mod shop opens and additional shells, sticks, buttons, membranes and accessories are purchasable, it might be improved. Regardless, it still stands highly recommendable as a controller.
