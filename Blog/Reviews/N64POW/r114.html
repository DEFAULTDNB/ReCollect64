N64 USB-C PSU

Overdue Upgrades

The Nintendo 64 hardware is now over 30 years of age, and a startling era of recomps, ports and FPGA devices is in full swing. Regardless, it doesn't mean that you can't still use your existing hardware with a few new modern tweaks to make your experience more in tune with modern sensibilities.

My personal OG system set up now contains several QOL upgrades that never existed in the late 90s. For example, I have an ED64-Pro flashcart, an EON Super 64 HDMI adaptor, the Hyperkin Hall-Effect Stick replacement for my OG controller, and now I have an updated power supply that runs off the same cable I use for both my Nintendo Switch 2 and my two FPGA consoles, the A3D and the M64.

Box Clever

This review is going to be short but incredibly sweet, because at its core, this is merely a power supply, so how much can be said about that? Not much in all honesty, but that's actually a good thing!

The N64 Pow is a USB-C-powered replacement PSU for original Nintendo 64 hardware that allows you to power your N64 via a USB-C cable. The device weighs just 32 grams and measures up at an astonishing 3.7 cm x 4 cm x 3.5 cm deep, slotting effortlessly onto the power connector on the rear right of the console as you look at it. The device is an impressive 90% smaller volume than that of the original NUS-002 PSU.

POW Block

The trick with the Linhexis N64 POW, which cost me just £16 a couple of weeks ago (but now seems to be hovering around the £18 mark), is to purposely pair it with a high-quality power adapter and cable. The one I have in use is that of the Nintendo Switch 2's 20.0V / 3.0A (60W max) OEM power cable, which is overkill for this device, but I know that the NS2 cable safely supports this profile with ease and additional power delivery protection.

Delivering a steady 15-volt DC at 3.0 amps, I noticed absolutely zero heating up of the device, no plastic smell, and no degradation or issues when in use. I know that some off-colour brands heat up quickly and have been reported to have the tendency to melt or set your house on fire, but honestly, this combination has been serving me well for well over a week of daily play now, with deliberate testing of the device's temperature and potential flexibility after prolonged periods of use. Spoiler: there was none!

Though this item in particular has thoroughly impressed me I feel that I must impart a short warning. Please be careful of cheap knock-offs, and please please please do not use no-name brandless cheap cables or power adaptors that you get off Temu or extremely cheap shops on the high street, because these are most likely to be the culprits that fail, melt, or overload and cause your N64 to become damaged or destroyed.

The Pros:

 80% smaller than the OEM power supply
 Absolutely zero notable heat-up
 Plug-and-play ease of use
The Cons:

 Requires AC Adaptor and USB-C cable (not supplied).
 Be warely of cheap clones/power cables/adapters.
Conclusion:

I have to admit that with the decluttering of cables I'm currently going through, this 2-second PSU swap feels perfect for me. I now have one cable ready to power 4 devices and since this PSU also has power delivery for safe and reliable steady current that produces negligible heat (if any) during use, I'm satisfied to give it my seal of approval.
