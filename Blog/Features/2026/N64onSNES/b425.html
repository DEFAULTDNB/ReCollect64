N64 SNES Ports!?

Super Sixty-Four FX

Would you believe me if I said that AAA Nintendo 64 titles such as Mario Kart 64 and Super Mario 64 had been somehow ported and their raw assets used to run an effigy of their 64-bit counterparts on the SNES?

At this point in time, nothing should surprise us anymore. We have impossible ports and recomps coming out of thin air thanks to AI, yet GameOfTobi's projects don't use AI. Using a combination of human ingenuity and skill to design custom engines from the ground up, GameOfTobi can then use scaled-down and compressed assets from the original games decompilation placed into them to give the effect that you're more or less playing the original game in some capacity.

GameOfTobi has famously ported Nintendo's flagship N64 platformer to a couple of bizarre devices, such as the Apple Watch and the Nintendo DS, so porting to the Super Nintendo Entertainment System somehow organically feels like the next logical step for him. Interestingly, Super Mario 64 has pretty much become the new Doom, with it being ported to almost every console and platform imaginable to date.

(GameOfTobi wasn't the first to "port" SM64 to the SNES. RetroPorts did this slightly before GameOfTobi, however, RetroPorts does use AI, such as Clause Opus and Chat GPT Astra to achieve these.)

(RetroPorts used a voxel engine for the demake port of Mario Kart 64 to the SNES. Using AI and a commanche style voxel span kernel renderer 20 FPS is achieved in what is essentially and unreleased "experiment" of what is theoretically possible.)

(RetroPorts recently previewed GoldenEye 007 running on a SNES via MESSEN. This port uses a polygon-based approach as well as fog and occlusion culling techniques.)

(As a bonus, here is footage Super Mario 64 running on Neo Geo. Again, this is AI-led, and shows what AI can achieve quickly, but it is not a sustainable way to complete projects without stringent code verifications against real-world context at every step.)

Man Vs Machine

Personally, I will always prefer to observe the steady growth of human-led projects over explosive AI-led ones, because they aren't devoid of progression, achievement and feeling. When people simply throw credits and prompts at an assistant to "vibe-code", it's an unrewarding journey where nothing is learned by the "developer". GameOfTobi's projects are prime examples of this; you can see him progress from one technical success to another, pushing boundaries without the need to "creatively borrow code" or step on anyone else's toes.

Right now we are experiencing a boom of AI-generated output that strives to be "first to market" but are rarely followed up or improved in any meaningful way once they're cast into the public light. The majority of which are found to contain game-breaking bugs or authenticity-crushing hallucinations that cannot be easily rectified. Yes, AI can get any project off the ground in minutes with what appear to be great results on the surface, but it has to be noted that they rarely succeed in doing it properly unless steered by the hands of a capable and knowledgeable human driver.

While AI is unashamedly serviceable for low-end consumers, it is not great for developers or lifelong devout fans of specific titles or franchises. AI unscrupulously stands on the shoulders of previous people's work without credit, often botching code that they cannot understand themselves and putting legitimate developers into a position where they do not want to continue for fear of being callously ripped off. It feels like an impossible situation, but this is the state of play that we find ourselves surrounded by right now.

I believe that deploying AI in order to assist, is ok, and in the case of decompilations, this is clearly the perfect use to automate labourious and monotonous tasks which can only result in a verifiable 1:1 result once complete. I also believe that AI as a lead in any project, such as recompilations, is not suitable. The overall creativity and direction must come from a human who also fully understands the nuances of the code at hand and can actively step in to correct any jagged or confused interpretations that the artifical "intelligence" is bound to run into.
