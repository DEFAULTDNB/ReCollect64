N64 Emulators For PS3/PS5

Emulation Station

A fresh series of unofficial Nintendo 64 emulators have arisen for none other than Sony's PlayStation 3 (2006) as well as the PlayStation 5 (2020) consoles this week. Beginning with ZeBobbel's Cell64, which popped up less than a week ago, the native N64 emulator for the PS3 already sports box art and refined menus, as well as semi-functional native emulation on the Cell Broadband Engine-based system. Notably, the first wave of AAA N64 titles, including Banjo-Kazooie, Perfect Dark, Super Mario 64, Paper Mario, Mario Kart 64, and Ocarina of Time, are working to varying degrees, and even third-party titles such as Castlevania and WipeOut 64 are up and running with promise.

Cell64 is in the compatibility testing stage, and as an example, it runs The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time at 17 FPS, which is just 3 shy of the N64 version's 20 FPS during gameplay, meaning it runs 15% slower than it should currently. Games like Perfect Dark are also running at around 17 FPS, though that game should run around 25 FPS, so it's running significantly slowly currently, around 32%, which is far more noticeable during gameplay. Other games exhibit broken effects and stuttery sounds but this should all be tweaked and improved upon on a game-by-game basis over time.

Playing With Power

PlayStation 5 emulation is taking a slightly different approach. The core emulator here is PS4-based, not native PS5 code, so while it technically would work on the PS4, it's actually the raw horsepower of the PS5 that accelerates this emulator's ability to run games at playable speeds. When run on the PS4 and PS4 Pro consoles, this emulator reportedly runs 50% slower than it does when run on a PlayStation 5 via boosted backwards compatibility.

No footage, core information or compatibility examples exist for this emulator yet, but Markus assures me that the AI generated assets should be looked at (I asked for removal and replacement), and according to his tweets, a release should hopefully come today, if not as soon as possible. I can only hope that this emulator also runs on a MupenPlus-based core and that it contains tweakable options and configurations.
