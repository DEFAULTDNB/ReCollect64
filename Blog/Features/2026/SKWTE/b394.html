Spaghetti Car-Banana

A web-based track editor has been released by Chris Gilpin that makes crafting your own tracks for Mario Kart 64's PC port SpaghettiKart a massively simplified experience. By modifying an existing course or starting from scratch, you can edit tracks, add walls, water, item blocks and more, all from the comfort of your favourite internet browser, and you can even give it a brief test ride before exporting it ready for racing.

— Chris Gilpin (@christophergilp) August 13, 2026
 

The MK64 Track Editor is a browser tool for authoring custom courses for SpaghettiKart (including this repo’s 8-player fork). You draw a path, paint surfaces, place props, and export a standard .o2r mod the game can load.

Upload a Mario Kart 64 ROM you legally own (or the mk64.o2r extracted from it) to browse and preview real track textures. This editor never provides a ROM.

Tracks stay on your machine unless you save or export — those copies can also be archived on this site so the author can review community work.

Created by Chris Gilpin (@christophergilp). Built for people who want to make Mario Kart 64 tracks without a full C toolchain.

A Subtle Skidding

These tools are undoubtedly built using AI, but the net result is a plethora of possible tracks that could be unironically entirely handcrafted by MK64 enthusiasts. The fact that AI was used to bring this track editor to life will likely be hotly debated and contested, but it might help people who are just entering into modding to at least gain a light grasp on something far bigger before turning to suites like DeadHamster35's ROM editing tool, Tarmac64.

Love it or hate it, the use of AI is becoming more widespread, even on a daily basis, with ports, tools and projects springing up overnight thanks to the speed at which AI can put things together. I will never sensationalise or endorse AI-led projects over human-led ones; however, as a news site, I am just here to share the news that they exist within the N64-centric news space and to allow my readers to make up their own minds whether things like this are going to be useful or not within the modding community.
