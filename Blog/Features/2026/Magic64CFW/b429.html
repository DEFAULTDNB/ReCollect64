Another M64 CFW WIP

Cores 'n' More

Nate, AKA CursedToastSDA, has recently revealed some features of his own M64 CFW that are under development. Best known for his ChroMagic CFW for the ModRetro Chromatic, Nate has taken to social media to show what has been incorporated into his custom firmware for the 64-bit FPGA console.

Starting out with RetroAchievements, Nate provided a short video that shows all 114 RetroAchievement trophies available for Super Mario 64 and the RAM hooking system accurately working on the M64 hardware. Whether this feature requires an online connection as standard or if an offline database needs to be accessible on the console's storage remains to be seen.

Added RetroAchievements to my firmware for the @modretro M64. "Here we go!" https://t.co/ljf4YADgWM

Secondly, he showed off a new core. This core was none other than the original PlayStation, booting the BIOS as well as running Resident Evil: Director's Cut. Reading between the lines of both of these feature announcements, yes, booting these games means that ISO and ROM loading without the need for a flashcart has been implemented. I haven't seen anything of the saving mechanics, but I hope for C-Pak saves as well as a save state system akin to memories on the Analogue 3D.

The M64 hardware could become quite the emulation powerhouse, with more developers showing more cores running on what was previously a realm exclusive to N64 cartridge playback. I would love to see niche systems like the Atomiswave, NeoGeo and CP System III (CPS3) emulated; however, we should realistically see 8-16-bit cores come to the system relatively quickly, but more complicated systems that are 32-bit and above will undoubtedly take more time.

Playstation? On my @modretro M64 firmware? It's more likely than you think. https://t.co/8iZVVf18sw

— Nate (@CursedToastSDA) October 9, 2026

Last but by no means least, Nate also demonstrated his ChroMagic CFW'd Chromatic connecting to the M64 and being used as both a Transfer Pak for N64 games like Pokemon Stadium and as a GB/GBC cartridge reader to play real Game Boy cartridges on the M64. This is a feature that ModRetro have themselves touted for the system but haven't released publicly yet.

There is a vast untapped wealth of possibility with the M64. It boasts a 16nm AMD Artix UltraScale+ FPGA paired with roughly 128 MB PSRAM (Pseudo-RAM) across four low-latency chips. Hopefully more cores, connectivity and features will come as this CFW progresses, but I already think that RetroAchievements and an additional PSX core is a stunning way to start. As long as the basics such as resolutions, filters, BT and WiFi connectivity and controller latency have been addressed foremost, and additional features like overclocking, game compatibility and virtual controller paks have also been rectified over that of the official M64 firmware's lacklustre offerings, Nate is on to a surefire winner.

In addition to the aforementioned features, the developer has also hinted at USB dongle support for increased controller compatibility as well as HID support for wired inputs too. The CFW is currently being polished up in the PSX core department and should be ready for a public beta very soon!

Man Versus Machine

From what I have read and understood, it appears that CursedToastSDA does not deploy any AI whatsoever in his code, so this could provide a human-built alternative to the recent OM64 CFW proposed by Fulvio (@fulviuus), which, though human-led, is underpinned by the use of AI throughout its core progression.

As divisive as the use of AI has become, I can say with an air of certainty that an entirely human-built CFW will be preferrably championed by the community. Nate is a senior software engineer who has the credentials and ability to make something wonderful here, so I have high hopes for this project (which I'm predicting might be named "Magic64 CFW"). I'm extremely happy to see more M64 CFW projects popping up; the more the merrier, but it will come down to a case of quality over quantity at the end of the day. For me, the most rounded and stable release will always command the most attention over a CFW that is a jack of all trades but a master of none with heaps more cores but more bugs and less quality control.

I will be providing updates and release notes here once it becomes public, and rest assured, I will be testing this out to the fullest once it is!
