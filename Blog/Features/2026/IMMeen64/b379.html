I.M. Meen N64 Port

Be Quick. Be Smart. Beware.

Educational games have never been the N64's strong suite. With just two mediocre Sesame Street edutainment titles featuring Elmo on the system dedicated to letters and numbers, the 1996 home console was not ideal for teaching kids anything other than how to smack Goombas in 3D, throw banana skins whilst go-karting, or time travel with fairies during its lifetime.

Just one year prior to the console's launch, in 1995, a Windows DOS game entitled I.M. Meen launched with the premise of teaching grammar to children through input prompts and 3-D mazes akin to those seen in Doom. The goal was escaping the "diabolical librarian", aka Ignatius Mortimer Meen's labyrinth, by identifying and correcting his spelling mistakes. These kinds of games were mostly prevalent in school, although my primary school only had BBC and Acorn computers, not Windows or DOS.

Following on from the 3D Pinball Space Cadet N64 port they released in April, Spooky Iluha has taken yet another game from 1995 and given it the libdragon/Tiny3D treatment, getting it to run at full speed and with optional mouse controls and rumble support, all within the confines of the Nintendo 64.

 

An Nintendo 64 port of I.M. Meen, built directly with libdragon and tiny3d.

The game targets a base 4 MB Nintendo 64 at 640x480i without zbuffer.

Controls:

D-pad / C-buttons: move

Analog stick: look

Z: punch or use the equipped melee weapon

A: interact

B: use the selected inventory item

L / R: cycle inventory

Start: pause

An N64 Mouse in controller port 2 can also be used: move the mouse to look, Mouse A to punch, and Mouse B to interact. A Rumble Pak in controller port 1 provides feedback for combat, interactions, traps, and death.

The pause menu opens the automap.

School's Definitely Out

As impressive as it is to see this ported to the N64, this is not a game that I think many will play. The target audience is quite niche, and the number of people who fall into its nostalgic circle who also own flash carts and are also willing to try this is probably quite minuscule. I personally think that this is a cool feat of coding, but personally I hope that more familiar or action-packed content gets ported to the N64.

I.M. Meen garnered poor review scores across the board, as it was deemed boring and failed to really teach anything of worth. However, if the animation looks oddly familiar, it's because the team behind I.M. Meen originally worked on the Philips CDi titles Link: The Faces of Evil and Zelda: The Wand of Gamelon in 1993. It would be interesting to see THOSE titles ported to the N64, for sure.
