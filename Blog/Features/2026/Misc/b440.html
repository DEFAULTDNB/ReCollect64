Two New M64 Releases Available

Body Harvest & Clay Fighter Sculptor's Cut ~ Nov 5, 2026

Dirty or Artistic: Clay

Last week, ModRetro added to their N64/M64 line-up by adding not one but two more titles to their inventory. Reproduction carts of the awesome 1998 3D blast 'em up Body Harvest (now in the hands of Piko) and the undeniably terrible fighting title Clay Fighter Sculptor's Cut received swanky red and purple cartridge releases, respectively.

Clay Fighter: Sculptor's Cut is one of the rarest and most sought-after games for true N64 collectors, as it was only released through Blockbuster Video, exclusively in the United States, and in limited quantities. The game commands an ungodly £900-1200+ price tag on second-hand selling sites for the unboxed cartridge alone due to its rarity (especially across the pond here in the UK), but this "re-release", along with all the other reproductions on ModRetro's site is priced at $49.99 (£46).

Modern Reproduction, Not Preservation

Having seen some posts online regarding this particular release, my feelings remain that these are simply reproductions which, I suppose, are nice for collectors or completionists within the bracket of collecting all of the current M64 releases, but this is not a way to honestly complete any ongoing N64 collection. In my eyes, these cannot possibly count towards a full set, because they simply aren't late 90s original in any way, shape or form.

Some people have already argued that this is an authentic, official re-release rather than a "repro" (reproduction), and though it is technically licensed by Interplay, what materialistically differentiates it from a bootleg on a flashcart? It's a modern copy of the game that doesn't contain any original hardware and is essentially the ROM burnt to a modern chip. The cart uses a custom shell colour that Nintendo has never offered or produced for the N64, and the labels are also not even original ones that have been miraculously salvaged from the annals of time.

Perhaps some might see this as similar to the Ship of Theseus paradox? If you dumped the official ROM 1:1 then proceeded to replace every individual part of the physical cart gradually over time as they deteriorated, including the battery (these are perishable so I will allow this one component), shell, labels, PCB, CIC and the main ROM chip (re-flashed 1:1), does this newly assembled version still constitute being considered the original? Conversely, if you gathered all the disregarded parts from that project and put them back together, wouldn't they actually combine back into being the original? Surely you would now have two physical carts, one built of original parts and one composed of entirely non-original parts.

Regardless, I digress. The games are boxed and come complete with a tiny little version of the manual ready to purchase on ModRetro's store, so it's ultimately up to you if you wish to indulge in these modern variants.
