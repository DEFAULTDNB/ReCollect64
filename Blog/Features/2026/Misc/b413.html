Super Mario 64 Zeebo Port WIP

It's-a me; Zeebo!

Nintendo 64's most iconic platformer, Super Mario 64, is getting yet another source port, but this time to a rather obscure Brazilian-exclusive digital-only home console, the Zeebo. Made by TechToy in 2009, the Zeebo was made to be a low-cost repurposed mobile hardware home console that was affordable and accessible, with only a 3G internet signal required to enjoy downloadable games.

Super Mario 64 was fully decompiled in 2019, and since then the source code has been ported to around 13 different platforms, including Linux and Android. Pedro, the talented developer of the Zeebo's GBC, SNES, and Genesis emulators, has turned his hand to porting the N64 title to the system, giving it a higher 640x480 resolution thanks to the more powerful 528 MHz ARM 11/Adreno 130 hardware paired with 160 MB of RAM the Zeebo sports over the N64's 1996-spec 99 MHz NEC VR4300/SGI Reality Coprocessor with just 8 MB of RDRAM at its disposal.

This is how the Zeebo port of Super Mario 64 runs right now, still some graphical glitches, but with a much better framerate and targeting 640x480 (the same resolution as the PS2/DC ports, double that of the original from N64, and higher than the resolution of the 3DS/PSP ports). https://t.co/mkjuSLdomS pic.twitter.com/RZE6hXYHEz
