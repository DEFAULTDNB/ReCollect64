M64 Special Announcement

Palmer Luckey's Live Demo ~ Jul 28, 2026

Hands-on Highlights

Palmer Luckey gave us a launch-day preview of the features of his latest retro console venture, the ModRetro 64. Known as the M64, Palmer was there to guide us through the features that AMD and the ModRetro crew had been beavering away at over the last several months.

Below is a quick breakdown of the key takeaways, and the most interesting tidbits:

Console Information:

+ Rock solid, dual tone plastic shells, with no bend or movement.

+ Disassembles with gamebit in under a minute, and reassembled in under two, with practise.

+ There will be an online parts store that sells shells, buttons, and components to modify and personalise your console.

+ It uses gold connectors for rear sockets as well as controller connectors, which prevents corrosion over time.

+ The RGB "light pipe" is a diagnostic tool as well as lighting for labels. It will glow red if the game or console has an issue.

+ There is a 360 degree lighting ring on the Pro Controller and around the controller ports.

+ SD card for firmware updates, screenshots and saves

+ HDMI port can be connected to the AV adaptor for S-video, composit, component (YPbPr), or RGB connectivity.

+ Eject button to mitigate cartridge strain on the board.

+ Mainboard is all opensource. Schematics available online and traces ready for modders.

+ No perishable hardware, no batteries, no capacitors, nothing to leak of damage hardware.

+ Passive thermal management system.

Controller Information:

+ M64 controller ergonomically optimal for changing hand position to play any game any way.

+ Redesigned to play games the way developers intended but scanned in based on OEM pad.

+ Authentic trident shape, and solves OEM issues by using robust aluminium and adding a ceramic coating to prevent slipping when sweaty.

+ Wider Z button is to prevent rocking.

+ The top has one seamless surface, rigid, great feel.

+ The controller purposefully has aesthetically pleasing internals that you can see.

+ The original silicon membrane bright pink, but you can swap out buttons and membranes on the parts store.

+ Passive ergonomics on controller buttons, to make them responsive and comfortable.

+ Uses BT 6.2 for reduced latency, just 0.375ms, which is 3x faster than previous BT standards, even with 4 controllers connected.

+ AA power pack, or recharge pack comes with the Pro Controller. Cable option is twice as long as OEM, and has clippable retainers to hold on to the controller.

+ Built-in rumble a is powerful eccentric rotating mass called "big rumble", which clones original style OEM rumble which is more authentic than using haptic or linear actuators.

+ The analogue stick utilises TMR (tunnel magneto resistance) as opposed to the original controller's optical encoder. It will have swappable sticks and gates and will have 4 adjustable tension levels.

+ The gate has a wider range of travel, no deadzone, is more robust and has less tilt angle.

Something Else..

Around two thirds of the way through the presentation, we received our early bird sign-up emails, stating that the console was ready to preorder with a $30 (£22) discount, and that the accessories and games would be available to order too.

The M64 itself is priced at £211, the Pro controller £83, and the games are priced at £46 each. With the £22 discount I scored the red AMD edition console and a matching controller for £303.56, inclusive of shipping and import duty fees.

Then followed gameplay of the four launch games Buck Bumble, Extreme-G: Turbo Fusion, Xibalba 64 and XenoCrisis. Each one looking stunning and demonstrating their appeal and variety within M64's catalogue of re-releases and remasters going forward. At the time of writing, there have been no remastered games released or announced yet.

I didn't notice much by way of the operating system or anything more towards the OpenFPGA architecture. I was hoping to see various modes and features previewed officially, though most influencers have shown a lot of these already on various platforms. Will the M64 be able to play other cores? I hope so, but this launch was not about any other consoles; it was strictly centred around their FPGA version of the Nintendo 64.

To end the presentation, Palmer did the Nintendo-esque "One more thing" to round it off. He then announced a gorgeous GameStop exclusive smoke grey variant of the console, which must have irked a lot of people, as we were all locked in and watching, eager to purchase at the first second, and boom, they got us, well, at least the Americans, who should have waited until the end to see if they wanted to hang on for another colourway first. Too late for many who don't wish to double dip, I think.

The smoke grey option is a tasteful one, but one I will never see in the UK unless the touted parts store brings those coloured pieces to my shores, because we do not have GameStop here. Having chosen the AMD red version, I might dabble in some customisation in the future, because the black button at least could do with changing out to red too.

Overall the "M64 Special Announcement" was a nice introduction to the hardware, with a nerdy deep dive into some of the nuances, and a great way to market the console by doing it mid "livestream" (I'm sure it was pre-recorded) and while everyone was hyped to oblivion. I noticed over 30k watchers at one point, so we were all very excited to see what they had in store, with our wallets ready, eager to pull the trigger on our next N64-centric FPGA system.
