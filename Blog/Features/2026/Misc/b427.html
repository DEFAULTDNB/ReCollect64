Incoming N64 to DC Ports

Racing to the DC

It appears that Dreamcast developer JNMartin84 has been quite busy of late, with several Nintendo 64 games, notably racing titles, getting source ports to Dreamcast. He has made no bones about his use of AI in the past, to assist workflows or even to help complete incomplete decompilations in order to get various games up and running on Sega's pre-Y2K system. Based on his previous works, I can only hope that these ones contain plenty of human-led, legible and verified code with zero Em dashes in sight.

First up we have demonstrations of Automobili Lamborghini, Extreme-G and The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (Master Quest). Automobili Lamborghini doesn't have a public decompilation, nor does Extreme-G, so these are interesting to see, though the performance on Extreme-G in particular is dishearteningly poor.

Next we have Beetle Adventure Racing (1999), the Volkswagen-branded racer that began life as a Need For Speed title, only to get swapped out for the lovable little new-style Beetles. The game appears to have a few graphical issues and some slowdown, but it is looking quite playable.

Next up we have Boss Game Studios' Top Gear Rally. The 1997 racer was the first N64 in the series and a follow-up to the SNES game Top Gear (1992). Again, this game is looking playable if a little slow. I'm not sure if it's just me misremembering the sensation of speed of this game though.

Next we have World Driver Championship (1999), the game that was meant to be the Nintendo 64's own Gran Turismo. This game is looking silky smooth, especially in high-res mode, and demonstrates one of the best-looking N64 racing games, making a superb transition to the DC hardware.

Last up on the list of new video demonstrations is Testrisphere (1997); the orbital game of blocks is deep in development and looking great! Dubbed "Testrisphere 128", the added resolution and horsepower of the Dreamcast definitely make this one to watch.

As a bonus, Wave Race 64 (1996) makes an fresh appearance on the Sega hardware after an initial announcement back in July. This port has been on my radar since it first surfaced, and I'm hoping that any serviceable human-made progress from the decompilation can/will be shared back into the mainline public decompilation effort to speed things along for legitimate non-AI-assisted PC and native port developers.

More, More, More

Additionally, but not shown working yet, are Dr Mario, Bomberman 64, Mega Man 64 and Aerogauge, which apparently all have varying degrees of issues from complete unplayable status up to "working decent in one mode but not the other". There are also further N64 decompilation projects ongoing, but no titles shared as yet. I wonder what they could be?

That completes my mini round-up of the known Nintendo 64 games that are currently winging their way to the Dreamcast. Personally, I thought that the FPS titles GoldenEye 007 and Perfect Dark would be exceptional candidates for the Dreamcast treatment, what with their AAA status and outstanding 4-player multiplayer that would transition well to the Dreamcast's four controller sockets.

Are there any that you would prefer to see? Let us know in the comments!
