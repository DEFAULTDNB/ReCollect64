GoldenEye 007 100% Decompiled

Preservative Measures

After roughly five years in progress, Rare's 1997 spy shooter GoldenEye 007 has successfully been 100% decompiled by KholdFuzion and co., exposing all the working code and microcode behind the landmark title, preserving it for generations to come, and opening the doors to a plethora of possibilities, such as more accurate and complex modifications (like Random-Eye-zer) and ports.

Notably, on the repository, the last commit is tagged "For England, James?", denoting that the project's climactic ending has come to pass. The site's tracking calculator is currently glitched and doesn't state 100%, though it finally is.

Recompilation Reassurance

There is already an official recompilation/port in the works, from reputable sources, so any concerns regarding "AI slop" ports can be allayed. This isn't to say that an AI-led port won't surface too now that the entirety of the source code is now legally in the public domain. In fact, one already exists and was recently demonstrated by Graslu00, exposing its inaccuracies and inconsistencies. The advice across the scene is to skip over these and hold on for the good human-built versions that are heavily in development and should be with us sooner rather than later.

GoldenEye 007 is one of those titles that I have been pondering about a port for the Dreamcast, specifically. The console has four controller ports and a tonne of potential, but it's only now, in recent years, that the N64-on-DC scene has truly flourished thanks to tireless decompilation efforts and developers such as JNMartin84, Bruce, and Falco_Girgis. I think GoldenEye's multiplayer mode could be a perfect fit for the system, alongside that of the Diddy Kong Racing port, which is looking incredible and is still in the works.
