SM64 Open World Tech Demo

Seamless Surroundings

Rovertronic, the Super Mario 64 modder extraordinaire has shown off his latest work, which is something quite special and unexpected indeed. Using BroDute's Star Revenge 8: Scepter of Hope's Gensokyo levels as a base, Rovertronic has expanded on the ROM hack's interconnected world idea and built a bespoke system that seamlessly streams in assets for each upcoming area, which gives the impression of a fully open world experience and not a single loading screens in sight!

The Star Revenge series of mods began in 2012 and progressed to the "interconnected" idea in the third game in the series in 2013. Most recently we saw a similar example of this with James Lambert's brilliantly crafted Junk Runner 64, a game that pushes the N64 to the limit with a massive sandbox environment for you to explore. The approaches and methods used here look quite different but essentially both utilise asset streaming, agressive memory management and culling to achieve something that surpasses our expectations of the 30-year-old 64-bit machine.

Technical Talk

With the N64 limited to a pool of just 8 MB of RAM (technically 9 MB; watch this), Rovert built a method of using direct memory access (DMA) on a low-priority CPU thread to make the magic happen. Where loading screens used to reside, there are none; instead, there are simple tunnels linking the areas together. You can also see that there is no cheating going on, no resetting coordinates or cleverly hidden portals linking things together; it's one congruent world!

Notably, there is 9.77% RAM remaining in this demonstration, and the memory pools for models and collision are yet to be optimised so there are a few micro-stutters here and there, but Rovertronic plans to iron those out with the remaining overhead in future iterations.

When this ROM hack will be fully playable is still up in the air. Hopefully it won't be too long before we see these stunning, human-made creations running on real OG hardware too!
