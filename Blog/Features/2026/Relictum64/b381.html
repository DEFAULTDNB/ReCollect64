Relictum 64 Beta

0xSmartis' Adventure Begins ~ Jul 31, 2026

Early Exploration

A couple of days ago, indie developer 0xSmartis released a first beta of his new first-person dungeon crawler Relictum 64 over on his Itch.io page. The game, which is in its extremely early stages, features basics such as text display, doors and keys, some searchable items, ladders and a compass, as well as containing proceedurally generated dungeons, multiple endings, adjustable in-game music/sfx, and it even packs rumble support too.

Relictum 64 essentially plays a lot like Shadowgate 64: Trials of the Four Towers, whereby you awaken in a strange place, you search around for items and keys, and explore your surroundings, solving puzzles along the way. The most interesting thing about this title is the traversal. Using a compas as a guide, you can either use the L or R shoulder buttons to rotate and look at each wall in a room or use left and right on the d-pad to scan around too. There is no 3-D movement other than rotating and selecting to go through a door or interacting with an item with the A button.

It should be noted that this is merely a beta test which is extremely short but interesting to digest in terms of Libdragon game development and N64 homebrew possibilities, nonetheless.

 

Relictum is a first-person dungeon crawler created for the Nintendo 64. Explore forgotten chambers beneath an old city, unlock sealed passages, uncover secret rooms, and survive the creatures guarding a buried past.

Three ancient scrolls are hidden within the ruins. How many you recover will determine what is revealed and how your journey ends.

Features include:

+ Atmospheric first-person exploration

+ Keys, secrets, traps, and hidden treasures

+ Multiple endings based on the scrolls you recover

+ A procedurally generated Hardcore mode

+ Persistent achievements and best times

+ Support for original N64 hardware and Ares

DISCLAIMER:

Even this project doesn't contain any output of generative AI, I used some in the beginning as placeholder art, which should be now completely removed. I also used my local LLMs to verify my code and teach me concepts during development.

Artificial Advancements

Previously expressing that he couldn't obtain art assets as quickly as the game could be coded, Matt (0xSmartis) openly admits the usage of generative AI to make placeholder images while his project was progressing. "AI art is already being used in AAA games and isn’t going away" was the sentiment he lamented in another post. This is something that we perhaps don't want to accept as game enjoyers and end users, but we have probably had it woven into the mainstream for far longer than we actually realise.

The use of AI to check code, suggest alternatives and produce placeholder images during production is possibly the best case here because there is no "slop" being blindly produced. 0xSmartis also verifies that all the finalised code is handmade, as are all the in-game assets and box art, which is great to have expressly confirmed prior to the game achieving a v1.0 release status.
